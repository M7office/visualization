    - Explain (with reference to material covered up to date, along with readings and other scholarly sources, as needed) why you classified that visualization the way you did.
      ```

Good visualization example: The Nobel Circle 

First, aesthetic quality; the visualization has a clear topic and strong framing. The title immediately tells the viewer what the visualization is about, and the summary numbers give useful context before the viewer explores details. This follows the principle that a good visualization should help the audience understand the purpose quickly.

Second, perceptural quality; the use of colour is meaningful rather than decorative. Colour is used to separate Nobel categories, which helps the viewer group information visually. This connects to the idea of pre-attentive attributes: viewers can notice colour groupings quickly before reading every label.

Third, exploratory composition; the visualization supports exploration. Because there are many Nobel laureates across many years, a simple static table would be overwhelming. The interactive format allows the viewer to inspect details, notice patterns, and move between overview and detail. This fits Munzner’s idea that visualization design should match the user’s task.


Bad visualization example: The Chinese Zodiac 

First, extraneous congtive load; the birthday input is confusing. The visualization does not clearly explain whether users should enter a Gregorian/solar-calendar birthday or a Chinese lunar/lunisolar birthday. This matters because Chinese zodiac years do not start on January 1, so some users may get the wrong result.

Second, perceptual quality; the compatibility panel is overcrowded with pictures. The images make the dashboard look interesting, but they also make it hard to identify the key information. Visual elements should support the data, not distract from it.

Third, source transparency; the visualization has unclear cultural terminology. “Chinese New Year” and “Lunar New Year” are related but not exactly the same. The dashboard should explain its terms and sources more carefully, especially when using culturally or geographically sensitive labels.
    







      ```
    - How could this data visualization have been improved?  
      ```
     The Nobel Circle：

     First, it could include a simple companion chart, such as a bar chart showing laureate counts by category, gender, or country. Cleveland and McGill show that position and length are easier to compare accurately than curves, angles, or areas. 
     
     Second, the visualization could improve accessibility by using a colour-blind-friendly palette and clearer labels or annotations for key findings.

     The Chinese Zodiac：
     First, the birthday input should clearly state which calendar is being used and provide automatic conversion from Gregorian date to Chinese zodiac year. 
     
     Second, the compatibility panel should reduce the number of images and use a simpler layout, such as a table, ranked list, or highlighted match score. 
     
     Third, the visualization should include clear source notes and definitions for terms such as Chinese New Year, Lunar New Year, and zodiac year.
      ```
